## Capture the iPhone clips

Shiven operates the tested phone; Manas calls the scene number and watches the checklist.

1. End any active iPhone Mirroring session before recording on the phone; Apple says screen recording and mirroring cannot run together.
2. Open Control Center from the top-right. If Screen Recording is absent, hold the background → Add a Control → Screen Recording.
3. Hold the recording control and leave its microphone off for silent app footage. Tap the control, wait for the three-second countdown, then return to ClearPath.
4. Start three seconds before the action. Perform only the planned taps, then hold still for three seconds. Stop using the red recording indicator. Review the clip in Photos before the next take.
5. Transfer the selected clips to the Mac using AirDrop or your existing trusted import method. Keep the originals until the finished export is checked.

These recording controls and automatic Photos saving are documented in [Apple's iPhone screen-recording guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iph52f6e1987/ios). Make a ten-second trial first to check readability and that no private notification appears.

## Edit and export in iMovie

Recommended default: iMovie on the Mac, since both presenters have access; no new service is needed.

1. Create a Movie project. Add the landscape 1080p introduction first, then the ten scenes in order. Import from your local raw-footage folder. The first clip determines project resolution/frame rate. [Apple's project setup](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie/create-a-new-movie-project-move6ccb3330/mac).
2. For portrait app footage, select the clip → Cropping → Fit. Keep the complete app frame on a dark landscape canvas. Do not stretch it or crop away simulation/source labels. Use a close crop for text only while keeping its provenance visible. [Apple's Fit controls](https://support.apple.com/en-my/guide/imovie/mova56757e7e/mac).
3. Trim each scene to its storyboard duration. Prefer hard cuts; one short dissolve into/out of the camera is optional. Drag the scene 9 face clip above the app clip → Video Overlay Settings → Picture in Picture; resize/reposition it in the empty margin → Apply. [Apple's overlay controls](https://support.apple.com/en-my/guide/imovie/mova1aaa682b/mac).
4. Mute raw app/code audio. Place each recorded voice segment under the relevant scene. Keep the camera audio for scenes 1, 9 and 10; do not layer a second spoken copy over it. Match volumes by ear, listen on headphones and a phone speaker, and avoid peaks/distortion. Music is unnecessary; add none unless you have rights and it does not compete with speech.
5. Add only the specified short captions at a readable size, high contrast and away from app controls. Keep a simulation caption throughout all fictional data, including any crop. Correct auto-caption spellings for Manas, Shiven, Frisco, Codex and Expo. Include the AI disclosure in both spoken scene 9 and written submission materials.
6. Watch the whole timeline without stopping. Confirm most of the cut is the working app (about 99/160 seconds if scene 9 retains app background), two code examples are legible, and names/roles match what each person actually did.
7. File → Share → File (or Share → Export File). Choose Video and Audio, resolution 1080p, High quality and Better Quality compression where offered. Next → save as ClearPath-CAC2026-1080p with the actual extension supplied by iMovie; do not rename a container to change format. [Apple's iMovie export guide](https://support.apple.com/en-my/guide/imovie/move6e0cb2ad/mac).
8. Open the exported movie in QuickTime. Use Window → Show Movie Inspector to check 1920×1080 and duration. [Apple's file inspector](https://support.apple.com/en-mk/guide/quicktime-player/qtp65619118c/mac). Watch it end-to-end; confirm audio, labels, private-data removal and no missing final frame. Inspect the actual file, not just the editing timeline. If 1080p is unavailable, establish a new project with a genuine landscape 1080p source clip first, then rebuild the cut.
